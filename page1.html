Techniques

Ceramic forms can be created using several established techniques, broadly divided into hand building and wheel throwing. Hand-building techniques — pinching, coiling, and slab construction — rely on direct manipulation of the clay and allow for greater control over irregular or asymmetrical shapes, making them especially accessible for beginners. Wheel throwing, by contrast, uses the rotational motion of a spinning wheel to produce symmetrical, rounded forms; it offers speed and consistency once mastered, but requires more practice to develop the muscle memory needed for centering and shaping. The following overview outlines four foundational techniques used throughout this practice.

The pinch method is one of the most fundamental hand-building techniques. A small ball of clay is shaped using the thumbs and fingers to form a hollow vessel. While simple in execution, this technique requires careful attention to wall thickness and symmetry, and remains a foundational skill in ceramic work. Pinch pots are well suited for small, organic forms such as bowls and cups, and are often the best starting point for beginners learning to feel how clay responds to pressure.

Slab construction involves rolling clay into flat sheets, which are then cut and assembled into the desired form. This technique is well suited for geometric shapes, trays, and structures requiring defined edges and consistent thickness, and allows for a high degree of precision. It works particularly well for square or rectangular forms, tiles, boxes, and pieces with flat panels or straight seams, where uniform thickness and clean lines matter more than rounded symmetry.
